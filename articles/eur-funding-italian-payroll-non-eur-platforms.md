# Funding Italian payroll in EUR from a non-EUR platform

> How non-EUR EOR and payroll platforms can fund Italian payroll in euros: who bears FX risk, where to convert, and what to agree with a local partner.

Italian payroll is paid in euros. Net salaries, social contributions and withholding taxes are all due in euros. If your platform holds client money in another currency, you carry an FX step between the client invoice and the Italian bank account. How you handle that step decides your margin, your cash timing and how often finance asks you questions.

This article covers the practical choices. It does not give rates or fees, because those depend on your banks and your volumes.

## Where FX risk actually sits

There are three moments where currency matters.

The client is quoted a price. The payroll is calculated in euros. The funds arrive in the Italian account and are paid out.

Between these moments the exchange rate moves. Someone has to own that movement. If nobody owns it on paper, you will own it by default.

The cleanest rule is simple. The euro payroll amount is fixed when the payroll is approved. Everything after that is a conversion problem, not a payroll problem. Do not let a rate change reopen a payroll that has already been approved.

## Three funding models

**Pre-fund in euros.** You convert ahead of time and hold a euro balance with your partner or in your own euro account. Payroll runs never wait for a transfer. The cost is that you tie up cash and take rate risk on the balance you hold.

**Fund per run.** You convert and send funds for each payroll after approval. You hold less cash and take less rate risk. The cost is timing. A late transfer becomes a late salary, and a late salary is the one failure your clients and employees will not forgive.

**Client funds in euros.** You invoice the client in euros, or the client pays into a euro account directly. The FX risk moves to the client. This is the simplest for you. It is also the hardest sell to clients who want one invoice in their home currency.

Most platforms end up with a mix. Large, steady clients get per-run funding. Small or new clients get an invoice in euros, or a deposit held in euros.

## Decisions to make before the first payroll

**Choose the quote currency and the rate source.** If you quote in the client currency, state which rate you use and when it is fixed. Name the source. A vague rule such as the rate on the day causes disputes later.

**Set a buffer.** Rates move between approval and settlement. A small buffer in your pricing absorbs this. Decide whether it is built into the price or shown as a separate line.

**Agree who pays conversion costs.** Bank fees and spreads add up. Write down whether they sit in your margin or pass through to the client.

**Define the cut-off.** Payroll approval, funding and payment each need a clear time limit. Share these with your Italian partner and keep them in the run calendar.

**Plan for corrections.** Retroactive changes, bonuses and final settlements create extra euro amounts after the main run. Decide how these are funded and at what rate.

## Reconciliation is where most pain appears

A euro payroll gives you euro totals. Your ledger may be in another currency. If the two do not tie out, finance spends days hunting small differences.

Keep three figures for every run. The euro amount approved. The amount received in the euro account. The amount in your home currency that you converted. Link them with a single run reference. Do this from the first run, not after the first audit.

Also keep the rate and the date it was applied. You will need both when a client asks why an invoice differs from a quote.

## Failure cases to plan for

A transfer that arrives short because of intermediary fees. A transfer that arrives late because of a bank holiday on either side. A rate spike between approval and settlement. A client who disputes the rate after the fact.

For each, decide in advance who covers the gap and how fast. A small standing euro float on the Italian side removes most of the stress. It lets payroll go out on time while you sort out the cause.

## What your Italian partner needs from you

Your partner needs a clear funding instruction for each run. That means the euro amount, the funding deadline, the reference and the account details. It also needs to tell you when funds have landed and when salaries have left.

This works best as structured data, not email. A machine-readable payroll summary and a machine-readable funding status let both sides reconcile automatically. They also give your finance team an audit trail without extra work.

## A short checklist

- Fix the euro amount at payroll approval.
- Pick a funding model per client type.
- Name the rate source and the fixing time.
- Keep a euro float on the Italian side.
- Tie every run to one reference across all systems.
- Agree in writing who pays for gaps and fees.

Partnership teams can request the data-exchange specification to see how payroll summaries, funding instructions and status updates are structured.

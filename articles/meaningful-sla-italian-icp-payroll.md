# What a Meaningful SLA for an Italian ICP Looks Like

> Payroll accuracy is not an SLA. Here are the measurable service levels a global EOR or payroll platform should ask of its Italian in-country partner.

Most payroll SLAs say the same thing. The partner will process payroll accurately. That sentence cannot be measured, cannot be audited and cannot be enforced.

If you run partnerships or operations at a global EOR or payroll platform, you need more than that from your Italian in-country provider (ICP). Accuracy is an outcome. An SLA should describe the behaviours that produce it, and the points where it fails.

This article sets out what to measure, and why each item matters in Italy.

## Why accuracy alone fails

Accuracy is usually reported after the fact. It is a lagging figure. By the time you see it, an employee has been underpaid or a contribution has been filed wrongly.

It is also hard to define. Is a payslip accurate if the net pay is right but the contribution breakdown is wrong? Is it accurate if the figures are right but they arrive after the pay date? A single accuracy number hides these differences.

Italy adds complexity. Pay depends on the national collective agreement (CCNL) that applies to the employee. Contributions follow social security rules that change. Local and sector rules affect what goes on the payslip. A provider can be accurate on average and still wrong where it counts for your client.

## The SLA components that matter

### 1. Input cut-off and confirmation

Define the date by which your platform sends changes. Then define how fast the ICP confirms that it received and validated them.

Measure the time from submission to confirmation. Measure how many input files are rejected and why. A partner that accepts bad data silently is worse than one that rejects it loudly.

### 2. Pre-payroll review delivery

The ICP should send a draft payroll for review before the final run. The SLA should state when it arrives and what it contains: gross to net, employer cost, and a list of changes against the previous period.

Measure on-time delivery of the draft. Measure how many items were flagged by the ICP before you found them yourself. That second measure shows whether the partner is actually checking.

### 3. Final file and payslip delivery

This is the date that employees feel. Define the deadline for the final payroll file, the payslips and the payment instructions, all in the format you agreed.

Track misses as a count and as a pattern. One late delivery is an incident. A repeated late delivery on the same step is a process fault.

### 4. Statutory filing and payment timeliness

Italian employers owe social contributions and tax withholdings on set deadlines. A late filing creates penalties and exposure for your client, even if the employee was paid correctly.

The SLA should require proof of each filing and payment, delivered on a stated schedule. Ask for the receipt, not a statement that it was done.

### 5. Error classification and correction time

Split errors by type: calculation, data, rule interpretation, filing. Each type has a different cause and a different fix.

Set a correction time for each severity level. An error that affects net pay needs a faster response than a cosmetic issue on a report. Measure time to acknowledge, time to correct and time to confirm the fix with evidence.

Also measure repeat errors. The same fault twice should trigger a root cause review, not another correction.

### 6. Rule change notification

CCNL renewals and contribution changes alter payroll. Your platform needs advance notice, not a surprise in the draft.

Define how early the ICP warns you of a change, in what form, and what the impact is on the affected employees. Measure whether notice arrived before the change took effect.

### 7. Reconciliation and reporting

The ICP should deliver a reconciliation between payroll, the general ledger input and the filings. Define the format and the deadline.

Measure unreconciled items at close and how long they stay open.

### 8. Support responsiveness

Define response and resolution targets for queries from your team and from employees. Separate urgent pay-date issues from routine questions. Measure both, and report them on a regular schedule.

### 9. Audit access

You need to see how a number was produced. The SLA should guarantee access to calculation logic, source documents and change history for any pay element on request, within a defined time.

## Make it measurable on both sides

An SLA that only binds the ICP will fail. Your platform controls the inputs. Late or incomplete data from your side causes many payroll problems.

Write the obligations of both parties. Define what counts as a valid input. Define what happens to service levels when your data arrives late. This protects the partnership and removes arguments at review time.

## Define consequences and review

A service level without a consequence is a wish. Decide in advance what follows a miss: a corrective plan, a service credit, an escalation path. Keep the structure simple so it gets used.

Review the figures on a fixed cadence. Look at trends, not just breaches. A metric that is slowly getting worse is the early warning you want.

## The point

Accuracy is what you want. These components are how you get it and how you prove it. Ask for the data behind each one, in a structured form you can load into your own reporting.

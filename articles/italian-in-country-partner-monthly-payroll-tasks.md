# What an Italian in-country partner does each payroll month

> The monthly task list of an Italian in-country payroll partner, from input cut-off to payment of contributions and taxes, seen from the partner's desk.

Global EOR and payroll platforms often see Italian payroll as a black box. Data goes in. Payslips and a payment confirmation come out. This article opens the box and walks through the month as the in-country partner lives it.

## Input cut-off

The month starts with a hard line. After the cut-off, new inputs wait for the next cycle. Without that line, nothing downstream can be trusted.

Before the cut-off, the partner chases the inputs it does not yet have:

- New hires and terminations, with the dates that drive pro-rata pay.
- Absences, sick leave and unpaid leave.
- Overtime and variable pay.
- Changes to bank details and family tax situation.
- Benefits and expense reimbursements that carry tax or contribution effects.

Most late inputs are not late because someone forgot. They are late because the platform and the local entity use different names for the same event. A good partner keeps a short glossary and applies it every month.

## Validation of the input file

The partner does not simply load the file. It reads it first.

Checks run against the applicable national collective agreement, the employee's level and the contract terms. A missing level, an unusual allowance or a start date that conflicts with the contract all get a query back to the platform. These queries are short and specific. They name the employee, the field and the question.

This step is where most errors are caught. A wrong figure found now costs a message. The same figure found after payment costs a correction run and an awkward conversation with the employee.

## Calculation

Italian payroll is rule-heavy. Contributions, withholding tax, regional and municipal surcharges, tax credits and conguaglio effects all interact. Collective agreements add their own rules on pay elements, notice and leave.

The partner runs the calculation, then runs it again from a different angle. Net pay is compared with the previous month. Every movement above a sensible threshold needs an explanation. If a net figure moves and nobody can say why, the run does not go out.

## Review and approval

The partner sends a pre-payroll report to the platform. It lists gross-to-net by employee, employer cost, and the exceptions found during validation. The platform's operations team reviews it and approves, or sends changes back.

Two habits keep this step short. First, the report format never changes without notice. Second, exceptions are listed at the top, not buried in the tables.

## Payslips and payment instructions

Once approved, the partner issues the payslips and the net pay file. Payslips must be clear to the employee. Italian employees read them closely, and queries about a single line are normal.

The partner also tells the platform how much cash is needed for net salaries and for the contributions and taxes. Treasury teams need these figures early. A late funding request is one of the most common causes of delay.

## Contributions and tax filings

After the run, the partner prepares the monthly social security declaration and the data for tax withholding. These are separate obligations with separate recipients and separate rules. Each needs its own check.

The partner also handles the other social insurance positions that sit alongside the main contributions. These are easy to forget on a platform dashboard. They are never forgotten in the filing.

## Payment through the unified payment form

The last task of the cycle is paying contributions and taxes through the unified payment form. The partner builds the form with the correct codes for each obligation, period and office. A wrong code does not always cause an immediate rejection. It can cause a mismatch that surfaces months later.

The partner then keeps proof of payment and reconciles it to the payroll run. That reconciliation is the control that lets the platform sign off the month with confidence.

## After the payment

The work does not stop at payment. The partner:

- Answers employee queries on payslips.
- Records corrections that must be applied in a later run.
- Updates the file for each employee, including changes in tax and family data.
- Keeps the audit trail: inputs, approvals, calculations, filings and payments.

Then the next cycle opens, and the cut-off comes again.

## What this means for platforms

Three practical points follow from the partner's desk.

1. Send inputs in a fixed, structured format. Free-text emails create manual work and manual errors.
2. Agree event definitions once. Hire, change and termination should mean the same thing on both sides.
3. Plan treasury early. Payment deadlines are fixed by law, so late funding is a platform problem, not a partner problem.

A stable monthly rhythm between platform and partner is worth more than any single feature. It makes errors rare and visible.

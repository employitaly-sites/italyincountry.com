# How to onboard a remote employee who lives outside a major city

> Residence data, local surtaxes and delivery logistics decide whether onboarding a remote hire in a small Italian town runs cleanly. A checklist for platforms.

Most onboarding flows assume the employee lives in Milan or Rome. Many remote hires do not. They live in small towns, hill villages and rural areas. The contract is the same. The data and the logistics are not.

This article covers what global EOR and payroll platforms should collect and check when the employee lives outside a major city in Italy.

## Start with residence data

In Italy, the municipality of residence drives part of the payroll. Collect it as structured data, not as a free-text address.

Ask for these fields separately:

- Municipality of residence, with its official name and code
- Province
- Street, house number and postal code
- Municipality of domicile, if it differs from residence
- Date the residence was registered

Small municipalities create problems that big cities do not. Names repeat across provinces. Some municipalities merged, and the old name still appears on documents. A postal code can cover several municipalities. If your form accepts free text, you will get mismatches.

Use a controlled list of municipalities. Validate the street address against it. Store the municipality code, not only the name.

## Why residence changes the payroll

Regional and municipal surtaxes on personal income tax depend on where the employee lives. Two employees with the same salary can have different net pay because they live in different places.

This matters more outside big cities. Rates differ between regions and between municipalities. Some municipalities apply a surtax, some do not. Some apply exemptions under conditions. You cannot assume that a rural municipality follows the same rule as the nearest large city.

For your platform, this means three things:

- Do not default to the rate of the regional capital.
- Look up the surtax rule by municipality code, and record the source and the date of the lookup.
- Recheck when the employee reports a move, and at the start of each tax year.

If the employee moves during the year, the change needs a clear effective date. Ask for it at the point of the move, not at the next payslip.

## Residence is not always where the person works

Remote employees often split time. They may live in a village and spend part of the week in a nearby town, or at a family home elsewhere. Ask which address is the legal residence and which is the place of work. Keep both on file. Payroll uses residence. Workplace safety and equipment delivery use the place of work.

Do not guess. If the employee is unsure, ask them to confirm with their registry record.

## Logistics that break in small places

The legal side is half of the work. The rest is practical.

**Equipment delivery.** Couriers in rural areas may have limited routes and longer lead times. Some addresses have no house number or use a local name. Collect a phone number and delivery notes. Offer a pickup point if the courier supports it. Start shipping before the first working day, not on it.

**Connectivity.** Check the internet connection before you promise a start date. A video-call-heavy role needs a stable line. Ask for the provider and the connection type. Plan a backup, such as a mobile hotspot, in the equipment list.

**Document signing.** Digital signature solves most of the problem. Still, some employees do not have a digital identity set up. Confirm early. If they need to visit an office to get one, the nearest office may be far away. Build that time into the plan.

**Health and safety.** Medical checks and training may require an appointment with a provider. In small areas, the choice of provider is narrow. Ask the employee for the nearest option and book it before the start date.

**Local support.** The employee may need help with tax, social security or registry questions. Municipal offices in small towns have limited hours. Give the employee a clear list of what to bring and what to ask, so one visit is enough.

## A short checklist

1. Collect residence and domicile as structured fields.
2. Validate the municipality against an official list and store its code.
3. Look up regional and municipal surtax rules by municipality, with source and date.
4. Confirm the place of work separately from residence.
5. Check the delivery address, courier coverage and a contact phone.
6. Check connectivity and plan a backup.
7. Confirm digital signature access before the start date.
8. Book health and safety steps early.

## What this means for partnership teams

The cleanest way to handle this is to exchange the data in a fixed structure. Your platform sends residence, domicile and place of work as separate fields with municipality codes. The local provider returns the applicable surtax rule, its source and the lookup date. Both sides keep the record.

This removes manual emails about addresses and rates. It also gives your payroll team a trail when a net pay figure is questioned.

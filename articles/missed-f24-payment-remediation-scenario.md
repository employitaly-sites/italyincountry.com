# A missed F24 payment: how to detect it, correct it and tell the client

> A missed F24 payment in Italian payroll, step by step: how to detect it, correct it voluntarily and keep the client informed.

A missed F24 payment is one of the more common operational failures in Italian payroll. It rarely comes from bad intent. A bank file is rejected, an approval stalls, or a funding transfer lands late. What matters is how fast you find it and how cleanly you fix it.

This scenario walks through the three stages: detection, voluntary correction and client communication. It is written for partnership, operations and payroll managers at global EOR and payroll platforms who need to know what happens behind the local entity.

## The scenario

A monthly payroll closes on time. Net salaries are paid. The F24 form, which settles withholding tax and social contributions, is prepared. The payment instruction is sent, but the bank rejects it. Nobody notices. The deadline passes.

The employees see nothing wrong. Their payslips are correct and their salaries arrived. The gap exists only between the employer and the tax and social security authorities.

## Stage 1: detection

Do not rely on the authorities to tell you. Notices arrive late, and by then the position is worse.

Build detection into the payroll cycle itself:

- **Reconcile every F24 against bank evidence.** Each prepared form needs a matching debit and a receipt. A form without a receipt is an open item.
- **Set a daily exception report after each deadline.** It lists forms prepared but not confirmed as paid.
- **Check amounts, not only status.** A partial payment looks like success in a status field. Compare the paid total with the calculated liability.
- **Keep a named owner for each open item.** An exception report that nobody owns does not help.

When the report shows a gap, confirm the facts before you act. Which form is affected? Which tax codes and contribution lines? Which period? Was the money never sent, or sent and returned? Write the answers down. You will need them for the correction and for the client.

## Stage 2: voluntary correction

Italian law allows a taxpayer to correct a late or missed payment on their own initiative. This is known as ravvedimento operoso. The employer pays the missing tax, interest for the days of delay and a reduced penalty. The reduction is larger when the correction comes sooner and before any inspection or notice.

The practical rule is simple. Act before the authorities do.

A clean remediation follows a fixed order:

1. **Freeze the facts.** Record the original due date, the missing amount and the cause of the failure.
2. **Calculate the correction.** Work out the tax, the interest and the reduced penalty for the actual date of payment. Have a payroll specialist or the responsible professional check the figures.
3. **Pay with the correct codes.** The correction is paid through F24 as well, with the specific codes for tax, interest and penalty. Wrong codes create a new problem.
4. **File the receipt.** Link it to the original open item and close the exception.
5. **Check the next cycle.** Confirm that the same cause does not repeat. If a bank file was rejected, fix the file path. If an approval stalled, fix the approval route.

Speed is the main variable. Every day of delay adds interest and may move the case into a higher penalty band. An EOR that waits for the next monthly close to fix a known gap is choosing a higher cost.

## Stage 3: client communication

The client is the foreign company that employs the worker through the EOR. They do not need a tax lecture. They need to know what happened, what it costs, and that it is under control.

Send a short written notice as soon as the facts are confirmed. Keep it plain. It should state:

- what was missed and for which period;
- the cause, in one or two sentences;
- the corrective action already taken or scheduled;
- the additional cost, and who bears it under the service agreement;
- the confirmation that employees are not affected;
- the preventive change you are making.

Do not wait until the correction is complete. Tell the client when you find the gap and again when it is closed. Two short messages are better than one long one sent late.

Be clear about cost allocation. If the failure was caused by the EOR, the EOR absorbs interest and penalties. If it was caused by late or wrong data from the client or the platform, say so and point to the contract terms. Avoid argument in the first message. State the facts and the proposed treatment.

## What platforms should ask their EOR partner

If you resell or embed EOR services, you inherit this risk in front of your own customers. Ask your partner:

- How do you reconcile every F24 against bank evidence?
- How quickly does an exception reach a named person?
- Who calculates and pays the voluntary correction?
- What do you send to the client, and when?
- Do you pass remediation status to us in a structured way, so our support team can answer customers without chasing?

The last point matters most for platforms. Your customer will contact you first. If your support team cannot see the status, they cannot answer.

Partnership teams can request the data-exchange specification to see how payment exceptions and remediation status are shared.

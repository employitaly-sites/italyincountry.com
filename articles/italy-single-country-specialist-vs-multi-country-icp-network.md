# Italy coverage: one specialist or a multi-country network?

> A global EOR or payroll platform must choose between an Italy specialist and a multi-country ICP network. Here is how quality and vendor count trade off.

Every global EOR and payroll platform reaches the same decision about Italy. Do you add a specialist who only does Italy? Or do you route Italian work through an in-country partner (ICP) network that covers many countries under a single contract?

Both models work. They fail in different ways. This article looks at the trade-off between quality and vendor count, and at what partnership, operations and payroll managers should check before choosing.

## What the network model gives you

A multi-country ICP network reduces vendor count. You sign a master agreement, run a single onboarding, and use a single integration pattern. Invoicing is consolidated. Escalation paths look the same in every country. For a team that already manages dozens of markets, this is a real saving in management time.

The network also helps with speed. When sales closes a deal that spans several countries, one partner can start them all together. You do not wait for separate vendor reviews.

The cost sits in the depth of local coverage. A network partner spreads its attention across many jurisdictions. Italy is rarely its core market. The people who handle your cases may also handle other countries. Local rules change, and the person who knows the change is not always the person on your ticket.

## What the specialist gives you

An Italy specialist works in one jurisdiction only. Its staff handle the same types of case every day. Collective agreements, social security registration, payroll inputs, visas and relocation steps are the daily routine, not a side line.

That shows up in a few ways:

- Fewer back-and-forth questions on edge cases.
- Faster answers when a payslip, a contribution or a contract clause looks wrong.
- Better advice before the hire, not only after the problem appears.
- A clearer view of which collective agreement applies to a given role.

The cost is another vendor on your list. You need a contract, a security review, an integration, an escalation path and a billing flow. If every country gets its own specialist, the vendor count grows fast and your operations team ends up managing the vendors instead of the work.

## Where quality is decided in Italy

Italy is not a market where a generic process works well. Pay and conditions depend heavily on the collective agreement that applies. Contribution rules, benefit structures and notice terms follow from it. A partner who treats these as a lookup table will make mistakes that only appear months later, in a payslip dispute or an inspection.

So the question is not whether a partner can run Italian payroll. Most can. The question is how they decide which rules apply, and how they show the source of each rule. Ask for that in writing.

Visas and relocation add another layer. They involve several authorities and sequencing that the hiring company cannot see. A network partner may subcontract this. A specialist usually owns it end to end. Ask who is actually doing the work and who answers when it stalls.

## A practical way to decide

Do not choose on principle. Choose on volume and complexity.

If Italy is a small share of your demand and the cases are simple, a network partner is usually enough. The saving in vendor management outweighs the loss of depth.

If Italy is a growth market for you, or your clients hire there with complex needs, a specialist earns its place. The cost of one wrong payslip or one delayed visa is higher than the cost of one more integration.

Many platforms end up with a mix. They keep the network as the default and add a specialist for Italy only. The specialist becomes the escalation layer and the source of rules. The network keeps the commercial relationship simple.

## Questions to put to any Italy partner

- Who performs the work, and are they employees of the partner or subcontractors?
- How do you determine the applicable collective agreement, and can you show the source?
- How do you track changes in contribution rules, and how fast do they reach payroll?
- What data do you need from us, in which format, and how do you return results?
- What does an escalation look like when a case falls outside the standard flow?

The fourth question matters more than it looks. Vendor count is mostly an integration problem. If a specialist accepts your data format and returns structured output, the extra vendor costs little. If it needs manual handling, the cost grows with every case.

## The trade-off in one sentence

A network lowers management effort. A specialist raises accuracy. Pick the model that matches how much Italy matters to your book, and make the specialist fit your integration rather than the other way round.

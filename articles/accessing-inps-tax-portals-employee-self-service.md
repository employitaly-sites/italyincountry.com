# Accessing INPS and Tax Portals: Helping Employees Self-Serve

> How global EOR and payroll teams can help employees in Italy get digital identity and portal access for INPS and tax services, and why it matters.

Employees in Italy can check their own social security record and tax position online. They can download certificates, view contribution histories and file requests. All of this sits behind a digital identity. For a new hire from abroad, getting that identity is often the hardest part.

This article covers the basics your operations team needs to know. It also covers where you can help without taking on work that belongs to the employee.

## Why portal access matters

An employee who cannot log in depends on your support queue for every small request. They ask for a copy of a certificate. They ask whether contributions were paid. They ask what a tax notice means. Each question becomes a ticket.

An employee who can log in answers most of these alone. They see the data at the source. That is faster for them and cheaper for you.

Access also builds trust. People like to check that their employer does what it says. The official portals let them do that without asking you.

## The two main portals

The social security institute, INPS, runs a personal area for individuals. There an employee can view their contribution record, download statements and use services linked to their position.

The tax authority, Agenzia delle Entrate, runs its own online services. These cover the personal tax record, returns, communications and certificates.

Both portals use the same family of login methods. Once an employee has a valid digital identity, they can usually reach both.

## Digital identity: the basics

There are several ways to authenticate. The ones your employees will meet most often are these.

**SPID.** This is a public digital identity issued by accredited private providers. The employee registers with a provider, verifies who they are and receives credentials. Those credentials work across many public services. Each provider has its own rules, its own verification methods and its own app.

**CIE.** This is the electronic identity card. It works as a login method when the holder has the card and a compatible phone or reader. Many foreign nationals do not hold it at the start of employment.

**CNS.** This is the national services card, a smart card with a certificate. It is less common for new arrivals.

Older portal credentials still exist in some cases, but the direction is clear. Public services are moving toward the unified identity methods. Plan your guidance around SPID and CIE.

## What blocks foreign employees

The same problems appear again and again.

- **No Italian tax code yet.** Most identity providers ask for it. The employee needs it before registration can finish.
- **Identity document type.** Providers accept certain documents and not others. A foreign passport may work with one provider and need extra steps with another.
- **Phone number and email.** Many providers ask for a mobile number and an email address that the person can access. A foreign number may cause problems with some providers.
- **Language.** Provider screens and support are mostly in Italian. Some offer English, but not every step.
- **Verification method.** Some providers verify in person, some by video call, some with a digital signature or an electronic document. Availability and cost differ by provider, so the employee should check current terms.
- **Residence status.** Where the employee is in the process of registering as a resident, some steps may have to wait.

None of these is a reason to give up. They are reasons to prepare early.

## What your team can do

You should not create identities on behalf of employees. A digital identity is personal. The employee must hold the credentials and control them. Your role is to prepare them and remove friction.

**Start before day one.** Tell new hires in the onboarding pack that they will need a digital identity. Explain what it is and why. Do not wait for the first payslip question.

**Give a checklist.** List what they need: the Italian tax code, a valid identity document, a mobile number, an email address and a quiet half hour. Keep it short.

**Describe the options.** Explain SPID and CIE in plain words. Tell them to compare providers on verification method, language and cost, and to read current terms on the provider site. Do not recommend a provider unless you have a reason you can defend.

**Show them what to look for.** Once they can log in, tell them which sections matter. For INPS, the contribution record. For the tax authority, the personal record and communications. A short screen guide saves many tickets.

**Set expectations on security.** Remind them never to share credentials, not even with HR or payroll. No legitimate request will ask for a password or a one-time code.

**Offer a support path.** Name who they can contact when registration fails. Make sure that person knows the common failure points above.

## Where this fits in your platform

Global platforms often show payroll data in one place. Employees in Italy will still want to see the source records. A good flow links to the official portals and explains what each one shows. It does not try to replace them.

It also helps to track onboarding status. Know whether each employee has obtained their tax code. Know whether they report having a working login. That lets your team step in only where someone is stuck.

## Keep guidance current

Providers change their steps. Portals change their menus. Treat your guidance as a living document. Review it on a regular schedule and after any reported failure. Link to official pages rather than copying their text.

## The practical outcome

Employees who can self-serve ask fewer basic questions. They find errors earlier. They feel in control of their own records. Your team spends its time on real exceptions.

The work is simple. Explain early, give a short checklist, protect their credentials and point them to the source.

# Hidden costs in Italian ICP contracts: fees to watch

> Which one-off and recurring fees tend to hide in Italian in-country partner contracts, and how EOR and payroll platforms can spot them before signing.

Most contracts with an Italian in-country partner (ICP) look simple on the first page. A set-up fee, a monthly fee per employee, done. The surprises sit further down, in schedules, definitions and side letters. This article lists where they usually are.

The goal is not to avoid fees. Partners need to be paid for real work. The goal is to know what you are paying for and to price it correctly in your own customer contracts.

## One-off fees to check

**Onboarding and set-up.** Ask what the fee covers. Some partners include social security registration, the employment contract and the first payroll setup. Others charge each step separately. Get a list of deliverables, not just a price.

**Contract drafting and changes.** A standard employment contract may be included. A contract that needs a bespoke clause, a different working time model or a sector-specific variation may be billed as legal work. Check who decides what counts as non-standard.

**Visa and work permit support.** If your clients hire non-EU talent, this is where costs grow. Look for separate fees for the application, for document legalisation, for translations and for appointments. Ask whether a refused or delayed application is billed again.

**Relocation services.** Housing search, tax code, residence registration and bank access are often sold as packages. Confirm whether they are optional and whether the partner subcontracts them. A subcontracted service often carries an extra handling charge.

**Termination.** Ending an employment relationship in Italy needs care. Partners may charge a fixed exit fee, an hourly rate for the procedure, or both. Ask what happens when a dismissal is challenged. Legal defence costs and settlement amounts are rarely covered by the base fee.

**Offboarding administration.** Final payslip, final settlement calculation, notices to the authorities. Some partners fold this into the termination fee. Others bill it on its own.

## Recurring fees to check

**Monthly per-employee fee.** Check the definition of an active employee. Is a person on unpaid leave, long sickness or parental leave still billable? A partner who counts every person on the payroll register will bill more than one who counts only those paid in the month.

**Minimum billing.** Many contracts set a floor on monthly invoicing, or a minimum number of employees. This hurts platforms with a small and uneven Italian headcount.

**Payroll processing extras.** Italian payroll has features that foreign teams do not expect. Additional monthly payments, severance provisions and sector agreement rules all affect the calculation. Ask whether off-cycle runs, corrections and retroactive adjustments are included or charged per run.

**Benefits administration.** Meal vouchers, supplementary pension funds, health funds and company cars each need setup and ongoing handling. Some partners charge a flat fee per benefit, others per employee. Ask for both models in writing.

**Reporting and data access.** Standard reports may be free. Custom exports, API access and data feeds into your own system are often priced as extras. For a platform, this matters more than almost any other line, because you need clean data every month.

**Indexation.** Look for a clause that lets the partner raise fees each year. Check whether it ties to a published index or to the partner's own judgement.

## Pass-through costs

The contract should separate the partner's service fee from costs paid to third parties. Social contributions, insurance premiums, statutory payments and authority fees belong to the second group. Three questions help:

- Is each pass-through item listed by name?
- Do you receive the supporting document?
- Does the partner add a handling charge on top, and if so, where is it stated?

If a clause says "other costs as incurred", ask for a closed list.

## Timing and currency

Payment terms can hide cost too. Early funding requirements tie up your cash. Currency conversion margins add a silent charge when you are invoiced in one currency and the partner pays in euro. Late payment interest and suspension rights deserve a read. A short suspension right can stop payroll, which is not a risk you want to carry.

## Exit and transfer

Check what it costs to leave. Some contracts charge for transferring employee records, for a handover period or for early termination of the agreement. If you may later move employees to your own Italian entity or to another partner, the transfer terms matter on day one.

## A practical checklist

- Request a full fee schedule with every line named.
- Ask for the definition of billable employee.
- Separate service fees from pass-through costs.
- Ask what is included in termination and in visa support.
- Check indexation, minimums and payment terms.
- Confirm what data you receive, in which format, and at what price.
- Read the exit clauses before the commercial ones.

A partner who answers these questions clearly is usually a partner who runs clean operations. A partner who avoids them is giving you information too.

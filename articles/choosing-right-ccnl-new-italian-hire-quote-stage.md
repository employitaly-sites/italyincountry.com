# Choosing the right CCNL for a new Italian hire: the logic an in-country partner uses at quote stage

> How an in-country partner proposes a CCNL at quote stage: the inputs, the order of checks, and the data a platform should send with each request.

A quote for an Italian hire depends on one choice more than any other: the collective agreement, the CCNL. It sets minimum pay, job levels, leave, notice, and many employer costs. If the quote assumes the wrong one, the number changes later. Your client notices.

This article describes how an in-country partner (ICP) proposes a CCNL at quote stage. It is written for partnership, operations and payroll managers at global EOR and payroll platforms who want fewer corrections after the quote.

## Why the CCNL is not a free choice

In Italy, the CCNL is tied to the activity of the employer, not to the preference of the employee or the platform. A company that works in a given sector normally applies the agreement for that sector. The job title matters, but the business of the hiring entity comes first.

For an EOR model this creates a question. The foreign client has an activity. The EOR is the legal employer. Which activity counts? The ICP has to answer this before proposing anything. The answer should be documented, because it will be asked again at onboarding and at any later review.

## The inputs we ask for

At quote stage we need a short, structured set of facts. More is not better. Missing fields are the main cause of wrong proposals.

- The client's main business activity, in plain words and with an industry code if the client has one.
- The role: title, main duties, and whether the person manages others.
- The work pattern: full time or part time, fixed term or open ended.
- The work location in Italy, including remote work from home.
- Whether the client already has Italian employees or an entity, and under which agreement.
- Any agreement the client has already signed in another country that it wants to mirror.

The last point is often misunderstood. A foreign agreement or policy does not replace the Italian one. It can inform benefits design on top of the CCNL. It cannot lower the floor.

## The order of checks

The ICP works through the same sequence each time. This keeps proposals consistent across clients and makes them easy to audit.

**First, the activity.** Map the client's business to the sector whose agreement fits. If the activity is clear, this step closes quickly. If the client spans several sectors, ask which one drives the role.

**Second, the role inside that agreement.** Each CCNL has its own classification of levels. Duties decide the level, not the title. A "manager" title on a person with no team and narrow duties often maps to a lower level than the client expects. A modest title on a person with real autonomy can map higher. Pay and costs follow the level.

**Third, the contract type.** Open ended, fixed term and part time arrangements each have rules in the agreement. Some agreements are more flexible than others on these points. Check the specific agreement, not general assumptions.

**Fourth, the compatibility with the client's pay offer.** Compare the offered salary with the minimum for the proposed level. If the offer is below the minimum, the quote must show the correct figure and flag the gap. Do not adjust the CCNL to fit the budget.

**Fifth, the extras.** Check supplementary items the agreement may add: additional benefits, welfare provisions, supplementary pension or health funds, and contributions to bilateral bodies. These change the total cost and are easy to miss in a rough quote.

## When two agreements look plausible

This happens. A software company hiring a sales person, a consultancy with a technical team, a manufacturer with a pure services office. The ICP does not guess. We present the primary proposal with the reason, and name the alternative with the reason it was set aside. The client or platform can then confirm the facts that decide it.

If the facts are still unclear, we say so in the quote. A clearly marked assumption is better than a confident error.

## What the quote should show

A good CCNL proposal inside a quote includes:

- the agreement proposed and the sector reasoning;
- the level proposed and the duties behind it;
- the minimum pay for that level, set against the client's offer;
- the cost items that come from the agreement;
- the assumptions, listed, so they can be confirmed or corrected.

Each item should carry its source and the date it was checked. Agreements are renewed, and minimums change. A quote without a check date cannot be trusted next quarter.

## Where platforms can help

Most corrections we see come from data that never reached the ICP. The platform had the client's activity, but the form did not pass it on. The role description was a title only. The remote work location was left blank.

A fixed data exchange fixes this. The platform sends the same fields on every request, in the same format. The ICP returns the proposal in a matching structure: agreement, level, minimum, cost items, assumptions, sources. Your team can then show the result in your own quote tool without rewriting it, and can see at once when an assumption is open.

This also helps your support team. When a client asks why the figure moved, the answer is in the assumptions, not in an email thread.

## A short checklist

- Capture the client's activity, not only the role.
- Describe duties, not only the title.
- Treat the CCNL minimum as a floor.
- State assumptions in the quote.
- Record the source and check date for every figure.

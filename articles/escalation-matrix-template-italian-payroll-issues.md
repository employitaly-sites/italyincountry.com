# Escalation matrix template for Italian payroll issues

> A practical escalation matrix for Italian payroll issues: named levels, clear owners and timeboxes that global EOR and payroll platforms can adapt to their SLAs.

Italian payroll issues rarely stay small. A wrong contribution code, a late collective agreement update or a rejected filing can reach the employee's payslip fast. Without a clear path, the ticket bounces between your support team, your local partner and the authorities' portals.

An escalation matrix fixes this. It tells everyone who owns the issue, who steps in next and how long each step may take. This article gives a template you can adapt.

## Design rules

Keep the matrix short. Long matrices are not read.

- Name levels by role, not by number. Names survive reorganisations.
- Give every level a single owner. Shared ownership means no ownership.
- Give every level a timebox. When the timebox ends, the issue moves up automatically.
- Separate severity from level. Severity says how bad it is. Level says who handles it.

## Severity classes

Use three classes.

**Blocking.** Net pay cannot be paid on time, or a statutory filing will miss its deadline.

**Degrading.** Pay is correct but a document, report or filing contains an error that needs correction.

**Informational.** A question about a rule, a calculation or a process. No money or deadline is at risk.

Agree these definitions with your partner before the first payroll cycle. Disputes about severity waste more time than the issues themselves.

## The levels

### Triage

**Owner:** your platform's payroll support agent.

**Job:** confirm the issue, collect the employee and entity identifiers, attach the payslip or filing in question and classify severity.

**Timebox:** set by severity. Blocking issues are acknowledged immediately. Degrading issues are acknowledged the same business day. Informational issues are acknowledged within the standard queue time.

**Exit rule:** if the agent can answer from the knowledge base, close it. If not, hand over with a complete case file.

### Specialist

**Owner:** the Italian payroll specialist at your local partner.

**Job:** reproduce the calculation, check the applicable collective agreement, contribution rules and tax treatment, and propose a fix.

**Timebox:** a fixed window per severity. Write it into the partner agreement. Blocking issues get the shortest window and are worked in parallel with the next level.

**Exit rule:** the specialist either resolves the case or states in writing why it needs a decision from above.

### Payroll lead

**Owner:** the head of payroll operations at the local partner, paired with your operations manager.

**Job:** decide on exceptions, approve off-cycle payments or corrections, and coordinate with the social security and tax channels where a filing is involved.

**Timebox:** same business day for blocking issues. Next business day for degrading issues.

**Exit rule:** a documented decision and a customer-facing message.

### Partnership owner

**Owner:** your partnership manager and the partner's account director.

**Job:** handle repeat failures, contract questions, service credits and root-cause reviews. This level does not fix payslips. It fixes the process that produced the error.

**Timebox:** review within the next operating review, or sooner if the same cause appears again.

### Executive

**Owner:** a named senior sponsor on each side.

**Job:** step in only when the lower levels have failed to resolve a blocking issue within its window, or when the customer relationship is at risk.

## Rules that make it work

**One case file.** Every level reads and writes the same record. No side emails. The file holds the identifiers, the calculation, the source rule and the decision.

**Automatic promotion.** When a timebox ends, the case moves up without anyone asking. The owner of the next level is notified at the same moment.

**Holiday and cut-off cover.** Name a deputy for every owner. Mark the payroll cut-off days in a shared calendar. Most blocking issues cluster around them.

**Customer messaging.** The platform owns the customer message. The partner owns the technical content. Agree a standard template for each severity so nobody improvises under pressure.

**Evidence for every fix.** Each resolution cites the rule applied and the date it was checked. This builds trust and makes audits easier.

## What to measure

Track a short list.

- Time to acknowledge, by severity.
- Time to resolve, by severity.
- Share of cases that moved up a level because a timebox ended.
- Repeat causes per quarter.

The third metric is the most useful. A high share means the timeboxes are unrealistic or the lower levels lack information. Fix the cause, not the number.

## Putting it in the contract

Attach the matrix as an annex to the service agreement. Include the severity definitions, the owners by role, the timeboxes and the promotion rules. Review it after each payroll cycle during onboarding, then on a regular schedule.

A matrix that exists only in a slide deck will not hold under pressure. A matrix that sits in the contract, the ticketing tool and the shared calendar will.
